Basketball stats quiz

Can you turn a box score into the right number? These eight original exercises use fictional box scores, so you can sharpen your basketball numeracy without spoiling a daily puzzle.

Play today's five NBA questions

Try each question before opening its answer. Keep your own score: one point for every correct answer. These practice exercises are separate from the timed daily game.

  1. A player makes 6 two-pointers, 4 three-pointers and 5 free throws. How many points did they score?

    Reveal answer

    29 points. Compute 6 × 2 + 4 × 3 + 5 = 29.

  2. A player makes 9 of 20 field goals. What is their field-goal percentage?

    Reveal answer

    45%. Divide made field goals by attempts: 9 ÷ 20.

  3. A player makes 5 of 12 three-pointers. What is their three-point percentage, rounded to one decimal place?

    Reveal answer

    41.7%. 5 ÷ 12 × 100 = 41.666…%.

  4. A team scores 108 points. It made 12 threes and 18 free throws. How many two-point field goals did it make?

    Reveal answer

    27. The threes and free throws account for 54 points, leaving 54 points from twos.

  5. A player's points across four games are 18, 24, 31 and 27. What is their scoring average?

    Reveal answer

    25 points per game. The total is 100 across four games.

  6. A player grabs 4 offensive rebounds and 7 defensive rebounds. How many total rebounds?

    Reveal answer

    11 rebounds. Total rebounds combine offensive and defensive rebounds.

  7. A player has 48 assists and 16 turnovers over a stretch. What is their assist-to-turnover ratio?

    Reveal answer

    3 to 1. 48 ÷ 16 = 3.

  8. A player has 8 made field goals, including 4 made threes, on 20 field-goal attempts. What is their effective field-goal percentage?

    Reveal answer

    50%. Effective field-goal percentage weights each made three by an extra half: (8 + 0.5 × 4) ÷ 20.
